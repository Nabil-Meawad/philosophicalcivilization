# Foundational Note I

## Introduction

This document does not provide foundational answers for the project.

It merely grants the project the right to ask the questions that it may spend a long time examining.

---

## 1. The Phenomenon

The project begins with the observation of a recurring phenomenon in human experience:

Human beings construct centers of meaning,
discover their limits,
and then return to constructing new centers of meaning.

The project does not assume a prior explanation for this phenomenon.
At this stage, it treats it simply as an open subject for philosophical inquiry.

---

## 2. The Philosophical Problem

The philosophical problem does not lie in the fact that human beings construct centers of meaning.

It lies in the fact that they return to constructing them after discovering their limits.

Why does consciousness reproduce what it already knows to be incomplete?

---

## 3. The Central Question

How can we understand the persistence of consciousness in reconstructing centers of meaning despite discovering their limits?

---

## 4. Open Hypotheses

The central question opens the possibility of investigating hypotheses related to:

* Difference
* Limitation
* Need
* Dependence
* Vital Space
* Relation
* Incompleteness

None of these concepts are treated as conclusions or first principles.

They remain working hypotheses, open to examination, revision, and abandonment.

---

## 5. Method

The project proceeds from the phenomenon before proceeding to the idea.

It distinguishes between:

* Phenomenon
* Problem
* Question
* Hypothesis
* Result

No hypothesis is treated as immune from criticism, revision, or rejection.

The project does not protect its ideas from criticism.

It protects criticism from its ideas.

The project does not ask its hypotheses to survive.

It asks its method to deserve survival.

---

## 6. Limits of the Document

This document does not present a complete theory,
a philosophical system,
or final conclusions.

Nor does it treat its hypotheses as truths or first principles.

Its task is limited to identifying the phenomenon, the problem, the question, the open hypotheses, and the method that will govern the inquiry that follows.

---

## Closing Statement

The project does not begin with the answer.

It begins with the question.

And it does not ask its hypotheses to remain.

It asks its method to deserve to remain.
