# Foundational Note 4

## MCD — From Meaning-Centered Theory to a Falsification-Ready Comparative Framework

**Foundational Research Note — Draft 1.0**

Foundational Note 4 documents a major methodological transition in the development of **MCD**.

MCD began with a meaning-centered hypothesis concerning the formation, limitation, and reconstruction of centers of meaning.

Through adversarial testing and comparative analysis, several of its initial assumptions were rejected or substantially weakened.

The present document records that process and establishes the current status of MCD as an **open, falsification-ready comparative research framework**, rather than as an independently established theory.

---

## Purpose

Foundational Note 4 serves four purposes:

1. To document the revisions through which MCD developed.
2. To distinguish **meaning** from **structure** as analytically separate but interacting levels.
3. To require identifiable mechanisms when claiming causal influence between them.
4. To state clearly what MCD currently claims — and what it no longer claims.

The note is therefore not an attempt to make MCD theoretically stronger by adding concepts.

It is an attempt to make the framework **more precise, more limited, and more testable**.

---

## Core Statement

> **MCD studies the dynamic relation between meaning and structure as distinct but interacting levels of social organization. It assumes no fixed causal priority between them and requires identifiable mechanisms to explain how changes at one level affect the other.**

---

## Current Research Status

**Research status:** Open / Falsification-ready / Non-dogmatic

**Theoretical status:** Independent theoretical status not yet demonstrated

**Methodological status:** Coherent and promising

**Next requirement:** Independent comparative testing against existing theoretical frameworks.

MCD remains subject to revision, reduction, or rejection if empirical research shows that its analytical contribution is not sufficiently distinct or useful.

---

## Main Analytical Distinctions

The current MCD framework distinguishes:

- **M — Meaning:** configurations of meaning, values, justification, and interpretation.
- **S — Structure:** institutional arrangements, power relations, resources, incentives, rules, and mechanisms of reproduction.

The framework does not assume that either level has permanent causal priority.

Possible relations include:

- **M → S**
- **S → M**
- **M ↔ S**
- temporal non-alignment between M and S

Causal claims require identifiable mechanisms rather than chronology alone.

---

## Three Analytical Roles of Meaning

MCD currently retains three analytical roles:

- **M1 — Meaning as Cause**
- **M2 — Meaning as Effect**
- **M3 — Meaning as Feedback**

These are analytical positions, not fixed ontological categories.

---

## What MCD No Longer Claims

MCD no longer claims that:

- meaning is necessarily the origin of structure;
- structure is necessarily the origin of meaning;
- meaning and structure necessarily change together;
- meaning and structure necessarily converge;
- structural persistence means persistence of the original meaning;
- meaning change automatically produces structural change;
- weakening structural reproduction necessarily produces meaning-led transformation;
- every society necessarily possesses a single center of meaning;
- MCD has already established theoretical independence.

These rejected or weakened claims are part of the research record.

---

## The Status of the Center of Meaning

The **center of meaning** remains an important concept in the history of MCD.

However, it is no longer treated as a universal foundation of social organization.

It is now considered a **special case** within a broader analysis of meaning–structure relations.

---

## Methodological Principle

MCD follows a simple rule:

> **A temporal sequence is not, by itself, a causal explanation.**

If a transformation of meaning is claimed to contribute to structural transformation, the mechanism connecting the two must be identified and examined against alternative explanations.

This principle is central to the falsification-ready character of the framework.

## Relation to Philosophical Civilization

Foundational Note 4 is part of the broader **Philosophical Civilization** research project.

MCD is treated within the project as an analytical framework, not as a closed philosophical doctrine.

Its continuity depends on its capacity to remain open to:

- criticism;
- comparative testing;
- conceptual reduction;
- empirical contradiction;
- revision;
- and, if necessary, rejection.

The continuation of the project must never become a justification for the continuation of an unsupported hypothesis.

---

## A Note on Research Integrity

This document records not only what survived testing, but also what did not.

The reduction of a framework is not considered a failure when the reduction results from serious criticism and evidence.

For MCD, **what has been rejected is part of what has been learned**.

---

## Closing Principle

> **MCD does not ask whether meaning or structure is the ultimate cause. It asks how their relationship changes over time, what mechanisms connect them, when their trajectories diverge, and whether each subsequently reshapes the other.**

**The question remains open.**

**And MCD remains open with it.**
