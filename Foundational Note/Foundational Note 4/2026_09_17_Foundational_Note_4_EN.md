# Foundational Note 4

## MCD — From Meaning-Centered Theory to a Falsification-Ready Comparative Framework

### Status
**Foundational Research Note — Draft 1.0**

**MCD — Meaning–Structure Comparative Framework**

---

## Abstract

MCD began from a broad hypothesis concerning the human tendency to construct centers of meaning, discover their limits, and reconstruct meaning in new forms.

Subsequent comparative and adversarial testing demonstrated that this formulation was too strong to function as a general theory.

Meaning cannot be assumed to be the origin of structure.

Structure cannot be assumed to be the origin of meaning.

Structural persistence does not necessarily require substantive meaning.

Meaning change does not necessarily produce structural change.

Meaning and structure do not necessarily change simultaneously or converge.

Accordingly, MCD is no longer presented as a meaning-centered theory.

It is reformulated here as a **Meaning–Structure Comparative Framework**: a research framework that treats meaning and structure as analytically distinct but dynamically interacting levels of social organization, without assuming fixed causal priority between them.

Its central methodological requirement is that claims of cross-level causation must identify observable mechanisms connecting changes in meaning and structure.

The present note records not only what survived testing, but also what failed.

---

# I. Origin

MCD emerged from a broader philosophical-civilizational inquiry into the relationship between human consciousness, meaning, institutions, and historical continuity.

Its initial intuition was:

> **Human beings construct centers of meaning, discover their limits, and reconstruct meaning in new forms.**

This formulation generated an important research question:

> **Why might consciousness reproduce or reconstruct meaning after discovering the limits of previous meaning structures?**

However, the subsequent investigation revealed that this question contained several assumptions that could not safely be generalized.

In particular, it implicitly privileged:

**Meaning → Structure**

as the fundamental direction of social organization.

The comparative tests required that assumption to be abandoned.

---

# II. The First Major Revision

The initial model implicitly suggested:

**Meaning → Structure → Crisis → Reconstructed Meaning**

The revised model recognizes multiple possible directions:

**M → S**

**S → M**

**M ↔ S**

**M ∥ S**

where:

- **M** = meaning configuration
- **S** = structural arrangement
- **M ↔ S** = reciprocal influence
- **M ∥ S** = differentiated or asynchronous change

The direction of causation is therefore not fixed in advance.

It must be investigated historically.

---

# III. Meaning and Structure Are Distinct

The foundational proposition that survives the strongest testing is:

> **M ≠ S**

Meaning and structure are analytically distinct.

### Meaning

Meaning concerns configurations of:

- values,
- justifications,
- interpretations,
- identities,
- normative expectations,
- conceptions of legitimacy,
- purposes and standards of evaluation.

### Structure

Structure concerns arrangements involving:

- institutions,
- distributions of power,
- access to resources,
- rules,
- incentives,
- coercive capacities,
- organizational arrangements,
- mechanisms of reproduction.

The distinction does not imply independence.

It means only that the two levels should not be collapsed into one another.

---

# IV. Meaning Is Not Necessarily the Origin

Several tests challenged the proposition that social structures necessarily originate in meaning.

Postwar Japan provided a particularly strong example.

Major institutional changes were imposed or initiated through structural and political intervention before a corresponding transformation of the wider social meaning had fully developed.

Likewise, the Chinese reforms after 1978 demonstrated that substantial structural transformation could occur while important elements of ideological and political continuity remained.

Therefore:

> **Meaning is not necessarily the first cause of structural transformation.**

---

# V. Structure Is Not Necessarily the Origin

The reverse proposition also failed.

The abolition of slavery, the American civil-rights movement, and other cases demonstrated that transformations in values, legitimacy, and political meaning can contribute to structural change.

But even here the relation was never:

**Meaning → Structure**

in a simple or automatic form.

Instead, the transition required mechanisms such as:

**Meaning → Legitimacy → Collective Action → Political Pressure → Institutional Change**

Thus:

> **Meaning can contribute to structural transformation without being sufficient to cause it.**

---

# VI. Structural Persistence Does Not Require Substantive Meaning

One of the strongest adversarial tests challenged the assumption that meaning is necessary for social persistence.

Market and institutional systems can reproduce themselves through:

- incentives,
- interests,
- habits,
- coordination,
- sanctions,
- coercion,
- organizational inertia.

Therefore:

> **Structural persistence ≠ continuation of meaning.**

A structure may survive while its original justification weakens or disappears.

Conversely, a meaning may remain powerful while the structure that once expressed it changes.

This requires a strict distinction between:

**Persistence**

**Legitimacy**

**Meaning**

**Transformation**

They must not be treated as interchangeable.

---

# VII. The Three Analytical Roles of Meaning

The investigation retained three principal roles for meaning.

## M1 — Meaning as Cause

Meaning may contribute to structural transformation.

Example:

**M → Mechanism → S′**

---

## M2 — Meaning as Effect

Structural transformation may generate or reorganize meaning.

Example:

**S → M′**

---

## M3 — Meaning as Feedback

Meaning produced or altered by structural change may subsequently influence the structure again.

Example:

**M → S → M′ → S′**

This third relation is particularly important because historical causality may change direction during the same process.

---

# VIII. The Central Role of Mechanism

The most resilient methodological proposition of MCD is:

> **Cross-level causal claims require identifiable mechanisms.**

Temporal precedence is not sufficient.

If:

**M₀ occurs before S₁**

we cannot automatically conclude:

**M₀ caused S₁.**

The investigation must identify what happened between the two levels.

For example:

**M → Collective Mobilization → Political Pressure → Institutional Reform**

or:

**S → Changed Interests → Practices → Reinterpreted Meaning**

The mechanism must be observable and independently investigated.

This requirement is central to MCD's falsifiability.

---

# IX. Temporal Asymmetry

M and S may change:

- simultaneously,
- sequentially,
- at different speeds,
- at different magnitudes,
- or independently for significant periods.

Therefore:

> **Temporal asymmetry between meaning and structure is an empirical variable, not an anomaly that must immediately be explained away.**

A historical system may exhibit:

**M₁ → S₀**

for an extended period,

or:

**S₁ → M₀**

before meaning subsequently adapts.

The existence of such asymmetry does not by itself establish causation.

It establishes a condition requiring explanation.

---

# X. Crisis and Reconfiguration

Crisis should not be treated as synonymous with collapse.

A crisis may produce:

- reform,
- adaptation,
- repression,
- negotiated settlement,
- reconfiguration,
- restoration,
- revolution,
- or collapse.

Therefore the earlier proposition that declining structural reproduction necessarily increases the probability of meaning-led transformation was rejected in its strong form.

The more defensible formulation is:

> **A decline in structural reproduction capacity may increase the openness of the meaning–structure relationship to reconfiguration, but it does not determine whether the resulting process will produce reform, transformation, restoration, or collapse.**

This remains a secondary working hypothesis, not a law.

---

# XI. Hypotheses

## H1 — Conditional Reconfiguration

> A decline in structural reproduction capacity may increase the possibility of reorganization between meaning and structure, but does not determine the direction or outcome of that reorganization.

**Status:** Weak / secondary working hypothesis.

---

## H2 — Mechanism

> **When change in meaning contributes to structural change, the transition must occur through identifiable mechanisms connecting the two levels.**

**Status:** Strongest surviving hypothesis.

It remains open to falsification.

---

## H3 — Asynchronous Change

> **Meaning and structure may change at different rates or degrees, and their non-alignment may persist without immediate systemic collapse.**

**Status:** Supported but bounded.

The framework does not claim that non-alignment is harmless or permanent.

---

# XII. Comparative Testing

MCD was subjected to multiple historical tests, including:

- British abolition of slavery,
- postwar Japan,
- China after 1978,
- the U.S. civil-rights movement,
- South Africa,
- Tokugawa Japan,
- the Soviet Union,
- India after 1991,
- Weimar Germany,
- Korea and Taiwan,
- Tunisia and Egypt,
- and the Mandal transformation in India.

The purpose was not to accumulate confirming examples.

The purpose was to discover where the model failed.

Several initial claims were consequently abandoned.

This is a constitutive part of MCD's methodology.

---

# XIII. Matched-Case Testing

Comparisons between relatively similar cases produced a particularly important finding.

### South Korea / Taiwan

Both experienced authoritarian rule, economic development, and democratic transformation.

Yet their transitions differed in the mechanisms connecting social and political change to institutional transformation.

South Korea exhibited stronger mass mobilization.

Taiwan experienced a more gradual and institutionally managed transition.

Thus:

> **Comparable changes in political meaning do not necessarily produce identical structural outcomes.**

---

### Tunisia / Egypt

Both experienced mass mobilization and regime collapse during the Arab uprisings.

Yet their institutional trajectories diverged substantially.

The differing roles of:

- military autonomy,
- civil society,
- political negotiation,
- institutional design,
- and competing centers of power

helped produce different structural outcomes.

Again:

> **Meaning alone does not determine structural transformation.**

---

# XIV. The Predictive Test

The framework was then subjected to a more demanding question:

> **Can MCD make a prediction before the outcome is known?**

The comparison between Indonesia and Egypt demonstrated a preliminary predictive possibility.

The working expectation was:

> If a new democratic meaning emerges but the old center of structural power retains a strong capacity for reproduction, meaning change alone should not be expected to transform the structure.

Conversely:

> Where democratic meaning becomes institutionalized through mechanisms that constrain the reproduction capacity of the old power structure, structural transformation becomes more plausible.

The comparison was consistent with this expectation.

However, this does **not** establish independent predictive power for MCD.

Existing theories of:

- civil–military relations,
- institutional change,
- elite strategy,
- civil society,
- and historical institutionalism

can explain much of the same result.

Therefore the predictive test is regarded as **promising but not uniquely diagnostic**.

---

# XV. Comparison with Existing Theories

MCD must explicitly recognize its intellectual neighbors.

In particular, theories of structuration already reject simple one-way relationships between actors and structures.

Historical institutionalism already analyzes:

- institutional persistence,
- path dependence,
- critical junctures,
- and historical sequencing.

Cultural sociology and related traditions already analyze:

- meaning,
- legitimacy,
- symbols,
- identity,
- and political action.

Therefore MCD cannot claim originality merely by stating that:

> **meaning and structure influence each other.**

That proposition is not sufficient for theoretical independence.

---

# XVI. What May Still Be Distinctive

The possible contribution of MCD is narrower.

It may function as a **comparative research protocol** that systematically requires the researcher to:

1. define meaning independently of structural outcomes;
2. define structure independently of meaning;
3. measure change in both;
4. establish temporal sequence;
5. identify mechanisms connecting the levels;
6. distinguish causal influence from chronological succession;
7. examine temporal asymmetry;
8. test whether structural change subsequently reorganizes meaning;
9. compare cases with similar initial conditions but different outcomes;
10. expose the framework itself to falsification.

This potential methodological contribution remains to be demonstrated empirically.

---

# XVII. What MCD No Longer Claims

MCD does **not** claim that:

- meaning is the foundation of every structure;
- structure is the foundation of every meaning;
- meaning is necessary for structural persistence;
- meaning change necessarily produces structural change;
- structural change necessarily produces meaning change;
- meaning and structure must converge;
- meaning and structure must change simultaneously;
- continuation of a structure means continuation of its original meaning;
- crisis necessarily produces transformation;
- decline of structural reproduction necessarily produces meaning-led change;
- every society possesses a single center of meaning.

These propositions were either rejected, weakened, or left unconfirmed.

---

# XVIII. The Status of the "Center of Meaning"

The original concept of the **center of meaning** remains philosophically valuable within the wider Philosophical Civilization project.

But MCD can no longer treat it as a universal analytical foundation.

It is better regarded as:

> **a special configuration of meaning organization that may occur under particular historical conditions.**

Possible societies may instead exhibit:

- plural meanings,
- competing meaning systems,
- procedural ordering,
- networks of meaning,
- or relatively minimal shared meaning.

MCD therefore studies **meaning configurations**, not necessarily a single center.

---

# XIX. Falsification Criteria

MCD should be considered weakened if future comparative research repeatedly demonstrates that:

### 1.
Meaning and structure cannot be reliably distinguished analytically.

### 2.
Mechanisms connecting the two cannot be independently identified.

### 3.
The framework adds no explanatory or comparative value beyond existing theories.

### 4.
Its hypotheses can accommodate every possible outcome without prior constraints.

### 5.
Its concepts must continually expand whenever contradictory evidence appears.

The fifth criterion is especially important.

> **Conceptual expansion must not become a method of immunizing MCD against falsification.**

If the framework requires a new variable for every failed prediction, the framework itself has failed.

---

# XX. Methodological Protocol

A future MCD study should begin at:

**t₀**

and establish independently:

**M₀**

**S₀**

and, where relevant:

**L₀ / F₀ / C₀**

After the relevant historical event or process:

**M₁**

**S₁**

are measured again.

The researcher then asks:

### Direction

Was the dominant sequence:

**M₀ → S₁**

or:

**S₀ → M₁**

or:

**M ↔ S**

or:

**M ∥ S**

### Mechanism

What observable process connected the changes?

### Time

What was the temporal lag?

### Feedback

Did:

**S₁ → M₂**

subsequently occur?

### Counterfactual

Would the structural change plausibly have occurred without the identified meaning transformation or mechanism?

Alternative explanations must be considered.

---

# XXI. The Reduced Core

After the entire process of testing and reduction, the core of MCD can be expressed in four propositions.

### 1.
**M ≠ S**

Meaning and structure are analytically distinct.

### 2.
**M ↔ S**

They may interact dynamically without fixed causal priority.

### 3.
**Mechanism matters**

Cross-level causal claims require identifiable mechanisms.

### 4.
**Temporal asymmetry matters**

Meaning and structure may change at different times and rates, and this temporal relation itself requires investigation.

Everything else remains secondary.

---

# XXII. Core Statement

> **MCD studies the dynamic relation between meaning and structure as distinct but interacting levels of social organization. It assumes no fixed causal priority between them and requires identifiable mechanisms to explain how changes at one level affect the other.**

---

# XXIII. Final Assessment

At this stage:

**MCD is not established as an independent social theory.**

That conclusion must be recorded explicitly.

Nevertheless:

**MCD has survived as a coherent comparative framework.**

Its strongest contribution is not a new universal law concerning meaning and structure.

Its potential value lies in disciplined comparative analysis of:

**Meaning**

→

**Mechanism**

→

**Structure**

→

**Feedback**

across time.

The framework therefore remains:

> **an emerging research framework, not a completed theory.**

Its future legitimacy depends on whether it can generate predictions, distinguish cases, and produce explanatory value that cannot be obtained as effectively through existing frameworks.

---

# XXIV. Relation to Philosophical Civilization

MCD should not be confused with the larger project of **Philosophical Civilization**.

The broader project asks philosophical and civilizational questions concerning:

- meaning,
- consciousness,
- historical continuity,
- transformation,
- limits,
- reproduction,
- and the human construction of centers of meaning.

MCD is narrower.

It functions as an analytical instrument within that broader inquiry.

The original philosophical question therefore remains open:

> **Why does consciousness reconstruct meaning after discovering the limits of previous meaning structures?**

MCD does not answer this question.

It helps establish a more rigorous environment in which possible answers can be tested.

This distinction must be preserved.

---

# XXV. Closing Principle

The most important outcome of this note is methodological rather than theoretical:

> **A framework should become smaller when evidence demands it.**

MCD began with a broad claim about meaning.

Testing forced it to surrender causal priority.

Comparison forced it to surrender universal explanations.

Adversarial cases forced it to distinguish persistence from legitimacy and meaning from structure.

Theoretical comparison forced it to acknowledge that much of its apparent novelty already exists elsewhere.

What remains is therefore deliberately modest.

But it is testable.

And that is sufficient reason to continue.

### Final formulation

> **MCD does not ask whether meaning or structure is the ultimate cause. It asks how their relationship changes over time, what mechanisms connect them, when their trajectories diverge, and whether each subsequently reshapes the other.**

**That question remains open.**

**And MCD remains open with it.**
